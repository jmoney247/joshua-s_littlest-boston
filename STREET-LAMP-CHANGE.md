# Targeted Boston street lamp replacement

The front-right rust-colored utility pole at Object649-local (230.08, -214.47) is replaced by bostonStreetLamp. The new assembly is two meshes, two local materials and 660 triangles, with no image assets, real-time lights or animation loop. It uses a narrow cast-iron shaft, decorative base and collars, four-sided lantern, amber emissive panes and a peaked cap. The rear pole at (-124.42, 254.21) is now also replaced by bostonStreetLamp2, sharing the first lamp's materials and pane geometry, with 672 triangles including an extra retained-sign bracket.

The source audit used raycasts and connected-component bounds to identify the pole, conduit, boxes, crossarm braces, insulators, three complete outgoing cable runs, and old green lamp including its separate glass lens. Exact original face ranges are recorded in src/street-lamp.js. Private geometry index buffers collapse only those triangles; vertex buffers and materials are not edited. Original face numbering remains stable for the existing sign overlays. No GLB file is changed.

Both birds perched on the removed front crossarm and their four wing meshes are hidden so they do not float. Their shared skeleton and other birds remain. The rear pole's three outgoing electrical runs, opposite stubs, insulators, boxes, braces, old lamp/lens, and associated outlines are independently isolated in rearFaces and removed. Building-mounted cables and trolley infrastructure remain. Existing Fenway and traffic signs keep their original placement and artwork, with small brackets connecting them to the replacement shafts.

Verified in local Chromium from the default, opposite/rear, left/right three-quarter and close views: no remaining old pole, insulators, old lamp lens or birds floating over its former crossarm. Lamp base sits at the existing sidewalk footprint and the lantern clears adjacent signs and buildings. The original building-mounted lanterns are unchanged.

Build passed. Earlier production checks covered assets/console, loading, fallback/retry, animation, orbit controls, visibility pause/resume, desktop/mobile and iframe. The latest production-bundle checks passed for lobster desktop/touch discovery, nearby-click rejection, drag suppression, once-per-session success, noninteractive teaser, animation, orbit, loading and fallback/retry. Rear, side and opposite views confirm the second lamp clears the street furniture and has no remaining utility-wire fragments. GLB, artwork and poster hashes match ASSET-MANIFEST.json.

The first-lamp change reduced observed loaded-view draw calls from 175 to 171; GPU textures remained 57. The second lamp and lobster add four meshes total, 2,232 triangles and two lobster materials, with no textures or real-time lights. No new performance pipeline or changes to scene lighting were introduced. The existing Vite large-bundle warning remains non-fatal.

The hidden-object target is solely bostonHiddenLobster, a two-mesh procedural lobster attached to the original animated body parent, replacing the moving coffee cup. It is 20% larger than the first lobster; the rear-pavement copy is gone. The cup geometry, materials and generated print texture are no longer created. Approved signage stays unchanged. Its hit test rejects occluded targets; pointer-down must begin on the lobster and movement beyond seven pixels cancels discovery, even if the pointer returns. Secondary touch cancels a pending tap. The hint says “Drag to explore · Click the hidden lobster 🦞”; success says “You found the lobster!” and “But did you find the Secret Base yet?” No repeating animation loop was added.

The reference photograph was used only to understand lamp style and was not copied into the project. Browser inspection hooks and test screenshots remain outside production files.
